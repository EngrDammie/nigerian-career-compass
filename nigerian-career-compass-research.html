The Nigerian Career Compass — Research & Strategy

A comprehensive research and strategy document for building a data-driven career guidance platform tailored to Nigerian secondary school leavers. Covers the education landscape, competitive analysis, product architecture, technical decisions, and a phased development plan.

I Landscape & Opportunity

01 · The vision restated

Transform the confusing, manual process of choosing a university course into an automated, objective, and highly personalised experience for every Nigerian school leaver.

The Nigerian Career Compass is a decision-support system that cross-references a student's academic reality (WAEC grades, JAMB score, preferred universities) with their professional aspirations. It replaces the "trial and error" approach to admissions with data-driven matching, reducing the number of students who end up in courses they are not suited for.

🎯 Core promise

Enter WAEC results → select preferred universities → receive curated, achievable course recommendations.

📊 Data foundation

WAEC grades + JAMB subject combinations + university-specific cut-offs + course requirements = match score.

🎓 Three tracks

Science, Commercial, Arts — each with distinct subject requirements, career pathways, and university options.

⚡ Speed & simplicity

Designed for mobile-first usage. Results in under 60 seconds. No psychometric testing required.

The one-sentence version: Every Nigerian school leaver deserves clear, data-backed guidance on which university courses they can realistically get into — before they waste a JAMB registration or a year of their life.

📋
The universal rule

Every university course in Nigeria requires five (5) credit passes (C6 or better) at not more than two sittings, including:

  • English Language — mandatory for ALL courses

Design Decision: Grade Input Method
Support both manual entry (dropdown per subject) and image upload (OCR for WAEC result printout). Manual entry is faster for MVP; OCR adds convenience but requires ML pipeline. Store grades as numeric values (1–9) internally for calculation. Validate that English & Mathematics are present and ≥ C6 (≤ 6).

Design Decision: JAMB Score Handling
The app should not require JAMB score for initial recommendations — many users haven't taken it yet. Instead: (1) Show courses they qualify for based on WAEC alone (O'level eligibility). (2) Add optional JAMB score field to filter by realistic cut-off probability. (3) Show "Reach / Match / Safety" tiers based on score bands. Store user's JAMB score if provided; use it to refine rankings.

05 · University admission mechanics

Admission is not just about meeting minimums. Understanding the mechanics helps the app give honest, actionable advice.

The three admission pathways

📝 UTME (Regular)

Standard route: WAEC + JAMB UTME + Post-UTME. Most competitive. All 170+ universities.

🔁 Direct Entry (DE)

For A'Level, ND, NCE, HND holders. Enter at 200/300 level. Requires JAMB DE registration. Different subject requirements.

🎓 Pre-Degree / JUPEB

One-year foundation programmes run by universities. Guarantee admission to host university if passed. Good fallback.

How CAPS works (Central Admissions Processing System)

  1. Candidate meets JAMB minimum + university cut-off + course cut-off.
  2. University uploads admission list to CAPS.
  3. Candidate sees offer on JAMB portal — must accept or reject within deadline.
  4. If accepted, admission is confirmed; if rejected, slot goes to next candidate.
  5. Candidate can only hold one admission at a time.

Post-UTME variations

TypeDescriptionExamplesWeight in Aggregate
CBT Aptitude TestComputer-based test on campus or designated centresUNILAG, OAU, UNIBEN, FUTA30–50%
O'Level ScreeningPoints calculated from WAEC/NECO grades onlyUI, UNN, ABU, FUTO (some years)100% (no test)
HybridCombination of O'level points + CBT scoreLASU, UNIPORT, DELSUVariable

Catchment area & Educationally Less Developed States (ELDS)

⚖️
Quota system affects real chances

Federal universities allocate: 45% Merit, 35% Catchment, 20% ELDS. A candidate from a catchment state or ELDS gets a lower effective cut-off. The app should ask for state of origin and factor this into "Match / Reach / Safety" classifications.

// Aggregate score calculation (common pattern) // Varies by university — this is a representative formula function calculateAggregate(utmeScore, postUtmeScore, olevelPoints) { // Example: UNILAG style (50% UTME, 30% Post-UTME, 20% O'Level) const utmeWeight = 0.5; const postWeight = 0.3; const olevelWeight = 0.2; return ( (utmeScore / 400) * 100 * utmeWeight + (postUtmeScore / 100) * 100 * postWeight + (olevelPoints / 40) * 100 * olevelWeight ); }

08 · Market gaps & opportunities

Where the white space is — and why Career Compass can own it.

✅ No WAEC-grade-based eligibility engine exists
✅ No unified university requirement aggregator
✅ No catchment/ELDS-aware matching
✅ No "Reach/Match/Safety" tiering for Nigerian context
✅ No career pathway visualisation per course
✅ No Direct Entry / JUPEB pathway guidance
✅ No parent/guardian view mode
✅ No offline-capable PWA for low-connectivity areas
❌ Data freshness: cut-offs change yearly
❌ JAMB IBASS not API-accessible
❌ University sites inconsistent
❌ Post-UTME formats vary wildly

The "Why Now" factors

  1. JAMB policy stability: 2026 policy meeting set clear minimums (150/100) and age floor (16). Rules are settled for near term.
  2. Smartphone penetration: >60% of Nigerian youth have smartphones; mobile-first is now default.
  3. Data cost decline: MTN/Airtel/Glo data bundles affordable enough for lightweight web apps.
  4. Trust in digital: Post-COVID, online registration (JAMB, WAEC, CAPS) is normalised.
  5. No dominant player owns guidance: Myschool owns practice; JAMB owns process; nobody owns decision support.

11 · Matching engine design

The core algorithm — transparent, explainable, and tunable.

Matching pipeline

1Eligibility FilterHard filters: O'level requirements met, track match, accreditation status
▼
2Score CalculationWeighted match score (0–100) per eligible course
▼
3Tier ClassificationMatch / Reach / Safety based on JAMB score + cut-off predictions
▼
4Ranking & BoostsPreferred unis, catchment advantage, career alignment
▼
5Explanation GenerationHuman-readable reasons for each recommendation

Eligibility rules (hard gates)

function isEligible(course: Course, profile: UserProfile): boolean { // 1. Track compatibility if (profile.track && course.track !== profile.track) { if (!isCrossTrackAllowed(profile.track, course.track, profile.waecGrades)) return false; } // 2. O'level requirements (ALL mandatory subjects must meet min grade) for (const req of course.olevelRequirements) { if (req.isMandatory) { const userGrade = profile.waecGrades[req.subject]; const altGrade = req.alternatives.find(alt => profile.waecGrades[alt]); const bestGrade = Math.min(userGrade ?? 99, altGrade ?? 99); if (bestGrade > req.minGrade) return false; // Lower number = better grade } } // 3. English & Mathematics universal requirement if ((profile.waecGrades.English ?? 99) > 6) return false; if ((profile.waecGrades.Mathematics ?? 99) > 6) return false; // 4. Accreditation if (course.accreditationStatus === 'denied') return false; return true; }

Match score components (0–100)

ComponentWeightCalculationRationale
O'level Grade Quality25%Average of relevant subject grades (normalised, better grades = higher score)Stronger foundation = higher success probability
JAMB Score Proximity30%If JAMB provided: 1 - |userScore - predictedCutoff| / 400. If not provided: neutral (50)Primary predictor of admission
University Preference15%+20 if in preferred list, +10 if same state, 0 otherwiseUser intent matters
Catchment / ELDS Advantage10%+15 if catchment, +10 if ELDS, 0 otherwiseReal structural advantage
Career Alignment10%+15 if matches preferredCourses, +10 if related, 0 otherwiseMotivation & persistence
Course Competitiveness Penalty10%Negative weight for ultra-competitive courses (Medicine, Law) unless user score is very highPrevents false hope

Tier classification logic

function classifyTier( userJamb: number | null, predictedCutoff: PredictedCutoff, isCatchment: boolean, isELDS: boolean ): 'match' | 'reach' | 'safety' { if (!userJamb) return 'match'; // Default when no JAMB score const effectiveCutoff = predictedCutoff.merit - (isCatchment ? 20 : 0) - (isELDS ? 30 : 0); const diff = userJamb - effectiveCutoff; if (diff >= 30) return 'safety'; if (diff >= -10) return 'match'; return 'reach'; }

Explainability — every recommendation shows why

✅
Transparent reasoning builds trust
  • Match: "Your WAEC grades meet all requirements. Your JAMB score of 265 is 15 points above the predicted cut-off of 250 for this course at UNILAG."
  • Reach: "You meet O'level requirements. Your JAMB score of 210 is 25 points below the predicted cut-off of 235. Consider as a stretch option."
  • Safety: "You exceed requirements comfortably. Your JAMB score of 280 is 50 points above the cut-off. Strong backup choice."
  • Ineligible: "You need Physics at C6 or better (you have D7). Consider [related course] instead."

    14 · UX principles & accessibility

    The brand system already encodes most of this. Here are the product-specific applications.

    Core UX principles

    1 Zero-friction entry

    No login to start. Progressive disclosure: 5 core subjects first, "Add more" for others. Smart defaults (track auto-detected).

    2 Honest, not optimistic

    Show "Reach" tiers clearly. Explain why a course is a reach. Never hide competition. "This course typically needs 280+ JAMB."

    3 Mobile-first, data-light

    Target <100KB initial JS. Works on 2G/3G. Offline-capable (cached course data). Touch targets ≥ 48px.

    4 Explainability > Black box

    Every recommendation shows the calculation. "You meet O'level reqs. JAMB 210 vs predicted 235 = Reach."

    5 Action-oriented results

    Each course card has next steps: "Check Post-UTME date", "Download past questions", "Compare with similar course".

    6 Inclusive language

    Pidgin option. "You fit study this course" not "You are eligible." Avoid academic jargon.
